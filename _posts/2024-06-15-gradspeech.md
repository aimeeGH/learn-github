Welcome, proud friends/family, distinguished faculty/deans, and our accomplished graduates, as we celebrate the UCLA Statistics and Data Science Department's graduating class of 2024! Thank you for this opportunity to speak here today.
First some stats:
* #1 department
* #1 public university in the nation
* department's 25th anniversary
* fastest-growing program at UCLA.

In the wise words of our founder, Professor Emeritus Don Ylvisaker, "The good of statistics starts with the people - mentors, students, colleagues, collaborators, friends. With such a long list, why would one choose to be something other than a statistician?"

Four years ago, the Statistics and Data Science department looked much different. Data theory had just become a major, we were known solely as the Statistics Department, and our first impressions of major classes like STATS 20 were under less-than-ideal circumstances. When I was deciding on colleges back in 2020, I wasn't able to visit campus, so I resorted to browsing Reddit, specifically r/ucla. 

Some professors in our department forgo anonymity. Two days before Decision Day, I messaged u/mileschen on Reddit. He responded, and within our 15-minute conversation, I had committed as a pre-stats major. That initial conversation with Professor Chen not only set the stage for my academic journey but also introduced me to a vibrant community of scholars. Our interactions, from classrooms to clubs, showed us the value of a supportive student community, as we shared our love for data.

We wandered the confusing halls of the Mathematical Sciences Building, trying to find Professor Lew's office. We shared Whine and Cheese with Professor Tsiang. We tried to keep up with Professor Christou during STAND hikes (as much as in his classes).
We waited anxiously for the department to open more spots in our classes as we refreshed myUCLA for the hundredth time and frantically emailed Jose. We got excited by a dataset with the capability to crash our laptops as we chaotically got our code to run at DataFest.
We put our statistics knowledge to work by calculating the minimum final score we needed to pass our classes and the maximum number of classes we could theoretically skip.

Two months ago, I volunteered for Bruin Day, where admitted students decide whether they want to spend the next years here. It was the ultimate full-circle moment, welcoming the next generation of baby bruins. It seems that everyone wants to be a data theory or statistics major these days! At no other booth, could you speak to faculty, undergraduate, and graduate students, and of course, receive a free rubber duck.

Many of us had unconventional starts to college and end it with a greater degree of uncertainty. Yet, even when the odds were stacked against us, we made it here today. Life cannot be mapped out by a simple linear regression or by calling the lm() function in R. As much as we try to fit a pre-determined model of success, goals, careers, ambitions, and priorities ultimately ebb and flow. If there's one thing I learned during my time at UCLA, it is that failure is always an option - after a STATS 100C exam, life can only go uphill from there!
As data science has become increasingly significant to our world, I am positive that our class will use our statistical power for good. After all, "Data is the new oil " and "Data scientists are the sexiest job of the 21st century."

Despite all these catchy buzzwords, it's no secret that statisticians and data scientists top the ranking of best careers year after year - in no other field is there a greater expected value of life satisfaction and a higher variance of paths taken. Interests of our graduates span from biostatistics, education, public policy, and AI, just to name a few. A handful will be furthering our field by pursuing master's and PhDs, others will be applying our knowledge in industry-facing roles, and some of us will be moving back home with our parents.

The number one question I keep getting is "What are you going to DO after graduation?" Notice how this is a different question than "Who are we going to BE after graduation?" Just for a moment, let's consider what it means to be a human-being, rather than a human-doing. Not all of us have an answer to "What are you going to DO?", yet we all have the opportunity to decide who we become after we leave UCLA. We can choose to be compassionate, driven, and genuine because who we ARE is the core of what we DO.

If AI takes all our jobs, we must start speaking about each other, not just for what we DO for work, but for who we ARE as humans. Sure, we DO work in statistics, but we also ARE student leaders who built more inclusive spaces, community members who volunteer weekends at the food bank, and lifesavers who write Pokemon-themed practice exams so that we can all ace our finals. We ARE parents and guests who will drop everything for our graduates, battle traffic on the 405, and be here for our biggest moments.

While we started college in the waiting room of a Zoom, we end it, here today, in the waiting room of the rest of our lives. I cannot wait to see what you all do, but most importantly, who we each become. Congratulations, graduates, and GO BRUINS!
